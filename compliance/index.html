      How the numbers are worked out

      • Compliance is the share of required values that are filled with a real value. The plan says which parameters each element type needs at each milestone (the LOD matrix), so the number of required values grows as firms model more and as each milestone asks for more. That is why compliance drops the week after a milestone even though nothing was lost.
      • Model rules (warnings, clashes, duplicate Mark, worksets) are pass or fail against the limit written in the plan. They are shown beside the goal they protect and are not averaged into the percentage.
      • Pace is the number of required values a firm filled in its last four working weeks, divided by four. Values that stopped meeting the plan (deleted, overwritten, copied with a duplicate Mark) are counted separately. A week with no upload counts as zero.
      • Needed pace is what the firm must fill each working week to reach the milestone target on time, assuming it adds no more elements.
      • The gate at the top is one answer: would this version pass the milestone? It lists the plan's own conditions, the target and each model rule, and which ones fail.
      • Wrong or empty: a required value that does not meet the plan is either empty or holds something that is not a real value (a placeholder such as "TBD", or a value outside the allowed list). The two need different fixes, so they are counted apart.
      • New elements arrive filled: of the values required on elements a firm added in its last four working weeks, the share that was already filled. A firm that fills as it models stays high; a firm that models first and fills later does not, and its backlog grows.
      • Milestones: a milestone is judged on the last version checked on or before its date. It passes when overall compliance reaches its target and every model rule passes.
      • Deficiencies: one line for each value check of a firm that is below the target, and one for each failing model rule. It is open since the first version of its current unbroken run. The closing date projects the last four working weeks forward, skipping holidays. Severity says how far off it is; urgency says whether time is running out.
      • Three levels: the page opens on the project: one card per firm, the firm that needs attention first. Opening a firm shows its summary, its chart and its items. Opening an item shows its whole history. The trail at the top leads back up, and the address keeps the level.
      • Late or ahead: the values a firm still has to fill for the target, divided by what it fills a week net of what it breaks, against the working weeks left to the milestone.
      • On track, behind, badly behind: badly behind when the firm is four working weeks or more late, does not reach the target at its pace, or has missed two milestones in a row. Behind when it is late by less, or has an item that is overdue or will miss the milestone. Otherwise on track.
      • Holds back: the plan says which roles use each check's data. A firm holds another back when one of its open items is data that the other uses. With nothing said in the plan, the line is left out.
      • Payment check: four conditions per firm, the same for every plan: its model was uploaded and checked in the latest period, its own compliance is at the target, no model rule fails on its elements, and nothing is overdue from an earlier milestone. The card lists what is missing. A rule the checker does not split by firm holds no single firm.
      • Step set by the plan: counted in milestones a firm missed in a row on its own elements. The steps are the plan's own wording; this page adds none. With no steps in the plan, the column is left out.
      • Holidays: weeks inside the agreed holidays in the plan have no version and are left out of every count, as on the Scorecard. They never count as a slow week.
      • A gap is never a zero: a version that could not be checked keeps its place with the reason, and the line breaks there.
      • Where the data would come from: one version per week on VIM Server, each built from the Revit models the firms uploaded, checked against the requirement file the BEP Builder exports. The example file is example-school.json, written by compliance/build_example.py.
      • Look: colours, type and sizes follow VIM's public style tokens in its open-source web viewer (vimaec/vim-web, MIT licence). VIM does not publish a component library for web dashboards, so the parts here are built to match that style. The layout follows the VIM Flex model-check screen design in this repository's docs/model-check-screen. The founder of OpenBEP works at VIM.
      • What other dashboards do: the second round of this page took ideas from model-checking and software-quality dashboards. The notes and sources are in compliance/RESEARCH.md.
      • Sharing: the address of this page keeps the version and the firm you chose, so a link opens the same view. "Print this version" gives a paper or PDF copy.
